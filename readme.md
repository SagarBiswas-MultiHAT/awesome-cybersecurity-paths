<a id="why-this-handbook"></a>
## 📖 Why This Handbook Exists

A lot of people get into cybersecurity with a vague goal like "learn hacking" or "work in security". Months later, plenty of them find out the job they pictured looks nothing like the real thing. Someone aiming for red teaming discovers they love the analytical side of threat intelligence. Someone who started in compliance ends up obsessed with malware analysis.

This handbook is meant to help you figure that out **before** you commit a year of study. It splits the field into three pillars and 36 roles, and it walks through every role the same way, so you can compare them side by side and choose based on how the work actually looks day to day.

**It's written for:**

- 🎓 **Students** who want a clear map of the field before picking a direction
- 🔄 **Career changers** coming from IT, development, networking, law, or something unrelated
- 🛡️ **Junior security professionals** deciding where to specialize next
- 🧑‍🏫 **Mentors and educators** who need a structured, free reference to point people to

## 🧩 What Every Role Includes

Every one of the 36 roles follows the same structure, so comparing two careers is a matter of reading two sections side by side.

<div align="center">

| Section | What you get |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | What the job involves day to day, in plain language |
| **Why It Matters** | The real-world impact and business value of the role |
| **Skills Table** | Key skills, expected proficiency (Foundational, Intermediate, Advanced), and why each one matters |
| **Tools Table** | The tools practitioners actually use, what they're for, and the skill level they need |
| **Real-World Scenario** | A realistic incident or engagement that shows the role in action |

## 📋 Track Your Progress

Reading about a career is the easy part. The [Career Readiness Tracker](TRACKER.md) turns this handbook into a checklist you can actually work through:

- **Phase 0:** Core technical foundations (operating systems, networking, scripting)
- **Phase 1:** Home lab setup and practice platforms
- **Phase 2:** Pick a pillar and go deep, with target certifications for each path
- **Phase 3:** Portfolio and professional presence

**How to use it:** fork this repository, open `TRACKER.md` in your fork, and change `[ ]` to `[x]` as you finish each item. Your fork becomes a public record of your progress, which is handy to share with mentors or recruiters.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

<details>
<summary><b>Is this cybersecurity handbook really free?</b></summary>
<br>

Yes. Everything here is released under the [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 License](LICENSE). You can read it, share it, translate it, or adapt it, including for courses and study groups, as long as you give attribution. An interactive edition is also available on [Vault Reader](https://sagarbiswas-multihat.github.io/premium-notebooks/viewer.html?book=choose-your-cybersecurity-path-wisely).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>I have no IT background. How do I start a career in cybersecurity?</b></summary>
<br>

Start with [Chapter 1](chapters/03-chapter-1.md) to understand the landscape, then go to [5.5 Foundation Skills Every Path Shares](chapters/07-chapter-5.md#55-foundation-skills-every-path-shares) and [5.4 Building Your Home Lab](chapters/07-chapter-5.md#54-building-your-home-lab). Networking, Linux, and basic scripting come before any specialization. The [Career Readiness Tracker](TRACKER.md) breaks this into concrete steps.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Which cybersecurity path has the most job openings?</b></summary>
<br>

According to the handbook's [path comparison](chapters/07-chapter-5.md#52-path-comparison-at-a-glance), defensive security has the largest volume of open roles, and SOC analyst positions are among the most accessible entry points. Offensive security is more competitive at entry level, but specialized skills command a premium. GRC is growing quickly, especially in regulated industries.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Do I need to know programming to work in cybersecurity?</b></summary>
<br>

It depends on the path. Offensive security needs programming, especially at advanced levels. Defensive security needs a moderate amount, mostly scripting for automation. GRC needs very little, since the work is analytical, legal, and advisory. See the [path comparison](chapters/07-chapter-5.md#52-path-comparison-at-a-glance) for details.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Which cybersecurity certification should I get first?</b></summary>
<br>

CompTIA Security+ is the beginner credential listed for all three domains in [Appendix A](chapters/08-appendices.md#appendix-a-certification-roadmap-by-domain), and both the defensive and GRC roadmaps start with it. The offensive roadmap starts with networking fundamentals such as Network+, then moves to eJPT and later OSCP. Check the certification roadmap inside the specific role you're targeting before you pay for an exam.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How long does it take to become job-ready?</b></summary>
<br>

There's no fixed answer, since it depends on your background and how much time you put in. The [learning roadmaps in 5.3](chapters/07-chapter-5.md#53-learning-roadmaps-for-each-path) are laid out as months 1-3 for foundations, months 4-8 for core skills, months 9-15 for specialization, and year 2 onward for advanced work. Treat that as a pace guide, not a deadline.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is it legal to learn ethical hacking and offensive security?</b></summary>
<br>

Learning is legal. Practicing on systems you don't own, or don't have written permission to test, is not. Use your own home lab and legal practice platforms such as TryHackMe, HackTheBox, and PortSwigger Web Security Academy. A full list is in [Appendix C](chapters/08-appendices.md#appendix-c-recommended-learning-resources).

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Can I translate this handbook or suggest a missing role?</b></summary>
<br>

Yes, both are very welcome. Translations, new roles, and tool or certification updates are all covered in the [contribution guide](.github/CONTRIBUTING.md). You can also open a [Role Proposal](https://github.com/SagarBiswas-MultiHAT/awesome-cybersecurity-paths/issues/new?template=role_suggestion.yml) directly.

</details>
